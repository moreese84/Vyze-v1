2.2 billion people can't see clearly

At least 2.2 billion people live with a near or distance vision impairment, WHO's current fact sheet states; in at least 1 billion of those cases it was preventable or is still unaddressed.

Cloud vision assistance built the wrong architecture for this user. The blind user in Malaysia needs the assistant to work in the pharmacy queue, on the rural bus, with 2 bars of signal or none, every single time.

Every incumbent requires at least one of: network, money, English, or their camera data leaving the device. That is the gap this chart reads down toward.

A

Most vision AI runs in the cloud. Without connectivity it does nothing at the exact moment it is needed.

B

Connectivity is who they are, not where they are. Data plans, coverage gaps, and per-minute cloud fees tax every ask.

C

Camera frames leave the phone. The most sensitive data a person can generate (surroundings, mail, medicine) uploaded to a server by default.

D

English is not their language. Speech in Bahasa Malaysia or Chinese is a second-class seam across incumbent products.

One tap. It speaks.

No menus. No reading the screen. One gesture, one spoken answer, built for a hand that cannot look.

1

Single tap — Look

Full scene description: what is in front of the camera, spoken in one or two sentences.

2

Double tap — Ask

Ask any question by voice about what the camera sees: "what is this", "read this label", "berapa duit ni".

3

Medicine, never guessed

Identifies medication from a curated local database, or says plainly that it does not know. No improvising on pills.

4

Light & color check

Is the room light on? Which of these two shirts is red? Answers cloud assistants fumble.

5

Date, time, battery

Deterministic instant answers computed locally, never asked of a language model that can invent them.

6

SOS, three taps held

One gesture under panic sends the alarm; no navigating an app while something is wrong.

Chart row 05safety doctrinevyze
Chart row 05 · Read this line
A wrong answer can hurt someone.

What we refuse

Currency and banknotes are never identified: a wrong guess costs real money to a fraudster. Medicines outside the curated database are never named: a hallucinated pill name is a poisoning risk. The dose from the local database is spoken verbatim; nothing else.

What we pin

Deterministic facts come from code, not from the model. Time, date, battery are computed; medicine dose is looked up verbatim. Every answer is bounded: the model speaks only from the scene in front of the camera, never freestyled world knowledge.

spoken script · medicine, unlisted package · en

"That medicine is not in my list, so I can't tell you what it is. Please ask someone with you, or scan it with a pharmacist."

spoken script · medicine, listed package · en

"This is paracetamol 500 milligram. The usual adult dose is one to two tablets every four to six hours, no more than eight in a day."

The refusal is the feature. Cloud assistants say something fluent; Vyze says something true, and the difference is why users and caregivers will trust it.

Chart row 10business modelvyze
Chart row 10 · Read this line
Free where it matters. Paid where it scales.
Individual
free · the user is never the product

The core assistant stays free for blind users — matching the moral position that it is wrong to charge a blind person to see, and matching the competitive reality against free cloud rivals.

  • Optional paid tier later: premium voices, advanced doc-reading, family sharing — only after retention is proven.
Institutional
the commercial engine · recurring

Sites that must provision assistive tech at scale — without per-seat cloud fees they can't afford:

  • NGOs & blindness associations: bulk deployment plus localized content packs.
  • Enterprise & government: accessibility compliance (e.g. PWD employment access) with a zero-data-leakage guarantee as the wedge.
  • Healthcare & pharma: licensed medicine databases per country — each new market adds the SKU.
Doctrine tied to the model: the individual free tier costs almost nothing to serve sustainably precisely because the architecture is offline — the marginal cost per user is the APK download, not a cloud GPU bill. Scaling the free tier does not scale cost.
